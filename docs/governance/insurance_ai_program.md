**What this is.** An outline of the written AIS Program the NAIC Model Bulletin expects an insurer to keep, filled in for the AI systems of this repository. No insurer has adopted it. Applicability of any guidance cited here depends on the institution and its risk profile. This project is a demonstration, not a compliance program, and nothing in it claims compliance with any regulation or guidance. All data are public (Lending Club, freMTPL2 from OpenML 41214/41215, UCI Statlog German Credit, the OFAC SDN list and OpenSanctions) or SYNTHETIC / SIMULATED (the document packets, the agent applications, the reviewer decisions, the monitoring stream's drift, the champion/challenger loans and their outcomes). No real applicant, policyholder, reviewer or decision is involved.

**Regulatory basis.**

- NAIC Model Bulletin, "Use of Artificial Intelligence Systems by Insurers" (adopted December 4, 2023; [bulletin](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/cmte-h-big-data-artificial-intelligence-wg-ai-model-bulletin.pdf.pdf)). Its text was **not checked against a local copy**: the scripted download returned HTTP 403 (docs/rag_design.md). This outline therefore uses only the summary in docs/build_prompts.md ("Regulatory references", checked against primary sources on 2026-10-10): insurers are expected to keep a written AIS Program covering governance, risk management and internal controls, and third-party AI systems, in proportion to the potential harm to consumers. The NAIC [adoption map](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/legal-adoption-map-ai-model-bulletin.pdf) dated October 8, 2026 lists 29 jurisdictions, and the adopted wording varies by state. No section of the bulletin is quoted here.
- New York DFS Insurance Circular Letter No. 7 (July 11, 2024), use of AI systems and external consumer data in insurance underwriting and pricing ([circular](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/circular-letters/cl2024-07)), checked against the local copy `data/raw/regulatory/nydfs_circular_letter_2024_07.html`: it names the adverse impact ratio among the statistical metrics an insurer may use, says an insurer may not rely solely on a third party's claim of non-discrimination, limits quantitative assessments to protected classes for which data are available or may be reasonably imputed, and adds a clause drawn from the NAIC bulletin on terms in contracts with third-party vendors.

## 1. AI systems in scope

The tiering rule is the bank pack's (`src/uwcopilot/governance/packs_inventory.py`), applied to every AI system so that controls scale with the potential harm to consumers:

- Inputs: the model's purpose as designed and whether it has exposure to a real book (SR 26-2, section III: purpose together with exposure determines materiality).
- Purpose consumer_decision (an input to a decision about one applicant or policyholder (credit, underwriting, pricing)): Tier 1 (high) as designed.
- Purpose financial_risk (measures or prices financial risk for a portfolio without deciding on a person): Tier 2 (medium) as designed.
- Purpose method_demonstration (demonstrates a method only; its output is never used for a decision): Tier 3 (low).
- Tier now: the tier as designed if the model has exposure, otherwise Tier 3. No model in this repository has exposure (no portfolio, no production use), so every model is Tier 3 now and is handled as an immaterial model: identified in the inventory and monitored for the conditions under which it would become material.
- This rule is a design choice of the project, not a rule from SR 26-2 or any other guidance.

| AI system | What it does | Tier as designed | Tier now | Status of its results |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Motor claim frequency, freMTPL2 | Expected claims per policy-year for one motor third-party liability policy. | Tier 1 (high) | Tier 3 (low) | measured |
| Motor claim severity, freMTPL2 | Expected average claim amount per claim on a capped basis; frequency x severity = expected loss. | Tier 1 (high) | Tier 3 (low) | measured |
| Fairness testing demonstration, UCI German Credit | The probability of bad credit, only so that approval rates per group can be compared (adverse impact ratio, equal-opportunity difference, proxy check). | Tier 3 (low) | Tier 3 (low) | measured |
| Document extraction (rules extractor; LLM extractor optional) | Reads SYNTHETIC document packets and fills application fields with page citations. | Tier 1 (high) | Tier 3 (low) | extractors run: rules |
| Underwriting agent (recommendation and memo; a human decides) | Runs allow-listed tools, applies the reference policy, drafts a memo and stops at the human checkpoint. | Tier 1 (high) | Tier 3 (low) | measured (deterministic metrics); LLM-dependent metrics not measured |

## 2. Governance

### Accountability and roles

- Owner of every AI system here: Rahul Vajja (portfolio project). There is no board, committee or second line: one person builds, tests and reviews, which is the main governance gap of this demonstration.
- Roles enforced in code (`security/rbac.py`): an `underwriter` approves up to a threshold and refers; only a `senior_underwriter` declines; only a `model_validator` promotes a model or signs an LLM migration certificate; an `admin` manages permissions and reads audit logs but can never approve, decline or promote. The agent's own role is never authorized for any of these actions.
- Roles are claimed in this demonstration (no identity provider): the code shows how authority is enforced once a role is known, not who someone is.

## 5. Fairness testing

- Claim frequency and severity: freMTPL2 has no protected attributes, so an adverse impact ratio cannot be computed: not measured. NY DFS Circular Letter No. 7 limits quantitative assessments to protected classes for which data are available or may be reasonably imputed. Region, Area and Density are geographic rating features that can act as proxies; a proxy assessment on freMTPL2: not measured.
- Fairness monitoring over time: not measured (no protected attributes in the monitored data).
- The method (adverse impact ratio, equal-opportunity difference, proxy check, each with a bootstrap 95% CI) is demonstrated on UCI German Credit, a different dataset (German credit data, not insurance).

Proxy check: how well the model's own features (protected attributes excluded) predict the protected attribute, as test AUC [95% CI]:

| Protected attribute | Proxy test AUC [95% CI] |
|---|---|
| sex | 0.704 [0.641, 0.766] |
| age_lt25 | 0.810 [0.750, 0.863] |

- Data-quality warning recorded with the demo: german.doc labels attribute 9 (personal status and sex) wrongly. The corrected code table published with the South German Credit data (Groemping 2019, Report 4/2019, Beuth University of Applied Sciences Berlin; UCI 2020, doi:10.24432/C5QG88) has the same row counts per code and gives: A91 male divorced/separated, A92 female non-single or male single, A93 male married/widowed, A94 female single. The 'sex' comparison follows german.doc (the convention in most fairness work) and so groups some single women as male and some single men as female; treat it as a demonstration of the method, not a measurement of sex disparity. 'sex_corrected_codes' uses only the unambiguous codes.
- Small test set: 300 rows, and the smallest test group in any comparison has 25 people, so confidence intervals are wide and a point estimate below 0.8 is weak evidence on its own.
- Fairness through unawareness: sex, age and foreign-worker status are not model features, but other features can act as proxies (see proxy_check). Excluding a protected attribute does not remove proxy effects.
- Attribute 9 mixes sex with marital status, and german.doc mislabels it (see data_quality_warning).
- The data has 1000 loans from one German bank, 1973 to 1975 (UCI South German Credit page); it says nothing about any current lending population.
- Groups can differ in their observed good rates (see observed_good_rate per group), so a gap in approval rates may partly reflect that; AIR alone cannot separate risk differences from unfair treatment.
